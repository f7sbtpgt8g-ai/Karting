2Mobile — at-track review and on-phone trace reading
Making traces readable on a phone
Values move off the axis and onto a fixed readout
Phone traces are too small to read a number off the plot. The header holds the values at the cursor for both laps, so the trace only has to show shape and the axis labels can shrink.
The finger never covers the trace
Scrubbing happens on a 52px bar in the thumb zone at the bottom, not by dragging on the plot. Corner-step buttons either side jump between turns, which is how a driver actually navigates a lap.
One channel deep, not four channels shallow
Traces are 2–3× taller than the desktop equivalent with heavier strokes (2.4–3.4px). Pedals collapse into one row. A whole-lap strip under the plots shows which slice of the lap is on screen.
Landscape is a real mode, not a fallback
Rotating gives the full lap at desktop stroke density with minimal chrome — the fastest way to read a whole trace at the track.

1Karting telemetry — web analysis + at-track mobile
Assumptions & direction
Who it's for
Club racer reviewing their own laps, and a coach comparing several drivers in one session. So: lap list always visible, driver switching cheap, no wizard flows.
Visual language
Broadcast timing conventions (purple = best, green = gain, red = loss) on a pit-wall dark workspace, with a light "factory technical" alternative. Mono/tabular numerals throughout, dense.
Placeholders
Track outline, corner names, lap times and setup values are stand-ins. Real circuit geometry, engine classes and MyChron/AiM channel names should replace them.
Variations focus on data layout: 1a stacks channels the way a data engineer reads them; 1b leads with the track map so a driver sees where the time went before reading a trace.
